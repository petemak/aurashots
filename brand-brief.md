# Aura Shots Brand Brief

Source of truth: `business-context.md` (read in full), plus the owner decisions passed with the brief: new tagline, British English only, prices in RwF, no newsletter, black and pink luxury. The logo file `business/images/logo/logo.jpg` was viewed.

## 1. Name and tagline

- Name: **Aura Shots** (given). The logo wordmark is the script "Aura shots" and the Instagram handle is @aura_shxts. In running text use "Aura Shots"; in the logo, keep the script as drawn.
- Tagline: **Not just photos…it's an aura**
  - Keep the ellipsis character (…) and the lowercase "it's", exactly as the owner wrote it.
  - The old slogan "Capturing moments" is retired. Do not use it anywhere, including the logo redraw.

## 2. Target audience

Stylish, image-conscious people in their early 20s to late 30s who want portraits, lifestyle shoots, couple and friend sessions, or birthday shoots that look editorial and cinematic rather than like a typical local studio. Today these are mostly Kigali locals who find Aura Shots through Instagram, TikTok and word of mouth. The new web shop is meant to reach two further groups who do not use social media to find photographers: urban professionals (for example, headshots and personal branding) and international tourists visiting Rwanda. All three groups value style and will pay for quality. Tourists and professionals need clarity and reassurance (what is included, price in RwF, how to book, the no-refund policy), while locals respond to personality and "main character energy". Visitors should feel they are entering a private, appointment-only creative experience, not a high-street studio.

## 3. Voice

Adjectives: **Confident, Elegant, Warm, Playful-bold** (a polished, editorial tone with a touch of main-character swagger).

- Confident: Do state plainly what is included and what it costs. Don't hedge or over-apologise.
- Elegant: Do keep sentences short and refined, with generous white space. Don't use slang overload, hype, emoji or exclamation-mark chains.
- Warm: Do speak to the client personally ("you", "your energy"). Don't sound corporate or cold, even in policy wording.
- Playful-bold: Do use the occasional vivid, cinematic phrase about light, colour and presence. Don't use clichés such as "capture every moment" (the retired slogan's territory).

Language rules: British English only (colour, personalise, programme, organise, "whilst" sparingly). Prices written as "50,000 RwF" style with the currency after the number, consistently. Dates in day-month-year. No newsletter wording anywhere (no "subscribe", "join our list"). Customers are contacted by email or WhatsApp after a request.

## 4. Colour palette

| Role | Name | Hex | Rationale |
|---|---|---|---|
| Primary | Noir | `#0A0A0A` | Matches the black field of the logo and the existing black-and-pink luxury identity. Near-black, not pure black, so photographs sit richly on it. Main background. |
| Primary | Aura Pink | `#E0457B` | A deep, slightly cool rose-pink: luxurious and editorial rather than candy or baby pink. Used for accents, links, buttons and highlights. The logo itself is monochrome, so this is a proposed exact pink; the owner should confirm it against their existing social media pink. |
| Primary | Pearl | `#F6F1EF` | Warm off-white for text on black and for light sections. Softer than pure white and echoes the white line-art of the logo. |

Accessibility notes (approximate WCAG contrast ratios):
- Aura Pink on Noir is about 5:1, so it passes for text and links.
- Pearl on Noir is above 15:1.
- White or Pearl text on an Aura Pink background is only about 3.9:1, which fails for small text. Use Noir text on pink buttons (about 5:1), or reserve pink fills for large type.
- Optional tint for subtle borders and hover states, not a primary: `#F4C6D7`.
- Photography should dominate. Keep colour use restrained so portfolio images carry the colour-theory work.

## 5. Typography direction

- Headings: **Cormorant Garamond** (600 weight; free on Google Fonts). Its high-contrast, fashion-magazine serif feel suits editorial luxury and pairs with the script logo without competing with it. Fallbacks: Georgia, serif.
- Body: **Inter** (400 and 500; free on Google Fonts). A clean, highly legible sans for prices, package contents, forms and admin screens, with good small-size rendering on mobile. Fallbacks: system-ui, sans-serif.
- Script: use only the logo wordmark. Do not set any live text in a script face.
- Self-host or subset fonts to keep page weight and hosting cost low.

## 6. Competitor and reference sites

Note: these were identified through web search results and listing snippets only. I did not browse the full sites, so check them before copying any pattern.

1. **Etsy** (https://www.etsy.com), named by the owner. Borrow: scannable product cards, clear price, clear "add to cart" action, simple guest-friendly flow, trust cues. Avoid: the busy marketplace density and many competing sellers. Aura Shots should feel like one exclusive brand, not a bazaar.
2. **Flytographer** (https://www.flytographer.com/destinations/kigali), a tourist-oriented photographer booking service that lists Kigali with prices (packages from about 385 USD). Borrow: how it explains what is included, the session length and delivery time, and the reassurance for visitors who do not know the photographer. Avoid: the generic, interchangeable marketplace look and the USD-only pricing. Aura Shots prices in RwF and sells a personal style.
3. **Kroma Portrait Studios** (https://kromaportraitstudios.mypixieset.com/Editorial/), editorial portraiture. Borrow: the "editorial study of identity" positioning, tiered packages that spell out looks, edits and creative direction. Avoid: the template-style Pixieset layout and the lack of any local cultural cue.
4. **Luxury Editorials** (https://luxeeditorials.mypixieset.com/services/), a clear services page with look, location and number of edits per package. Borrow: the exact package-content structure, which matches Aura Shots' own content list (edited pictures, videos, outfits, delivery time, locations). Avoid: the plain list presentation with little atmosphere.
5. **Kigali Center for Photography** (https://www.theeastafrican.co.ke/tea/magazine/rwanda-s-first-photography-centre-now-open--1378904), a local reference for the Kigali photography scene (found via news coverage). Borrow: the Kigali and Rwandan creative credibility. Avoid: the gallery and workshop focus, which differs from a paid private-session service.

## 7. Discrepancies and flags

1. **Slogan conflict inside the context file.** The "Existing Brand" section still says the slogan is "Capturing moments", while the "What we do" section and the owner decision use "Not just photos…it's an aura". Resolved in favour of the new tagline. "Capturing moments" is retired, including in the logo redraw. Note that the supplied logo.jpg still contains the old small slogan under the wordmark, so a new logo version is needed without it.
2. **Audience conflict.** The "Customers" section describes local customers only, while "What we do" says the new target is urban professionals and international tourists. The brief covers both and treats locals as the existing base.
3. **Customers "want to be informed about events and new services" versus the owner's "no newsletter" decision.** No newsletter is used. Updates stay on Instagram and TikTok, and the order confirmation by email or WhatsApp.
4. **Suspicious seed prices.** Besties-Package (12,000 RwF), Special Package (10,000 RwF) and Weekend Sessions (15,000 RwF) cost far less than the Single Session (50,000 RwF) and Couple Session (100,000 RwF), even though they are larger packages. They may be missing a zero (120,000, 100,000, 150,000?). The context file calls them editable seed data, so they were not changed. The owner should confirm. Brand copy should not quote prices until confirmed.
5. **Name casing.** "Aura Shots" (text), "Aura shots" (logo script) and "aura_shxts" (handle) all differ. The brief uses "Aura Shots" in text.
6. **Etsy as a reference versus exclusivity.** Etsy is liked for its ease of use, not its look. The tone is appointment-only and private, so only the interaction patterns are borrowed.
7. **Logo quality.** The supplied logo is a 150x150 JPG. The file already permits a faithful SVG redraw, with no new mark and no old slogan.
8. **Unresolved placeholders (<TBD>).** TikTok and Facebook links, delivery areas and fees, and the owner email are not given in the context file. They must stay as `<TBD>`.
9. **No-refund policy and policy texts.** The no-refund policy must be stated clearly and politely, and the legal text is placeholder wording for a human to review before launch.

business-context.md exists, so no assumptions were needed for the core brief. The following were my own choices and the owner should confirm them:
